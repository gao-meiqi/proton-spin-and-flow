\section{introduction}
In the grand architecture of the cosmos, the proton stands not merely as a constituent of the atom, but as the very heartbeat of visible matter. It is the first stable milestone of complexity in the universe’s evolution—a 1.6-femtometer sphere of chaotic, beautiful, and violent dynamics that gives rise to 99 percent of the mass of the observable world. To study the proton is to study the origin of "being" itself; it is the fundamental starting point where the abstract laws of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) manifest into the tangible reality of mass and structure.However, despite its ubiquity, the internal life of the proton remains one of the most enduring enigmas in modern physics. For decades, the "Proton Spin Crisis," sparked by the 1987 European Muon Collaboration (EMC) results, has challenged our intuition. We once believed the proton’s spin of $1/2$ was a simple summation of its three valence quarks. Reality proved far more poetic: the quarks’ intrinsic spins account for only about 30 percent of the total. The remainder must emerge from the orbital angular momenta of quarks and, crucially, from the gluons—the gauge bosons that are the "glue" of the universe.Gluons are not mere background force-carriers; they are the dynamic architects of the proton's interior. Their self-interactions and vacuum fluctuations create the "sea" from which the proton’s properties emerge. The gluon spin contribution, $\Delta G$, represents a direct window into this non-abelian vacuum. Yet, extracting $\Delta G$ from First Principles is notoriously difficult. On the lattice, gluon operators suffer from immense ultraviolet (UV) noise and complex renormalization patterns that involve mixing with quark operators.
In this work, we embrace the elegance of the Gradient Flow (or Wilson Flow) formalism to tame these fluctuations. By evolving the gauge fields along a fifth dimension of "flow time," we strip away the blinding UV noise, revealing the underlying physical signal of the gluon spin. This is more than a calculation; it is a meticulous deconstruction of the proton's soul. By matching these flowed operators to the $\overline{\text{MS}}$ scheme using state-of-the-art NNLO coefficients, we provide a precision bridge between the discrete lattice and the continuous reality of high-energy experiments. We return to the proton, the starting point of matter, to finally resolve how the "glue" helps define the spin of the world.
\section{the gluon part of the proton spin}
\subsection{emt methods}
The extraction of gluon spin originates from the Ji angular momentum decomposition, which relates the proton's total angular momentum $J$ to the matrix elements of the gauge-invariant Energy-Momentum Tensor $T^{\mu\nu}$. In this framework, the total angular momentum density is derived from the generalized angular momentum tensor:$$M^{\alpha\mu\nu} = x^\mu T^{\alpha\nu} - x^\nu T^{\alpha\mu}$$For the gluon sector, the angular momentum operator $\vec{J}_g$ is defined as:$$\vec{J}_g = \int d^3x \left( \vec{x} \times (\vec{E}^a \times \vec{B}^a) \right)$$where $\vec{E}^a \times \vec{B}^a$ represents the Poynting vector of the gluon fields, which is the $T^{0k}_g$ component of the gluon EMT.

On the lattice, we do not measure the spin $\Delta G$ directly as a local operator. Instead, we compute the Gravitational Form Factors (GFFs) of the gluon EMT. The matrix element of the gauge-invariant gluon EMT in a nucleon state is parameterized as:
$$\langle P', S | T^{\mu\nu}_g | P, S \rangle = \bar{u}(P', S) \left[ A_g(Q^2) \gamma^{\{\mu} \bar{P}^{\nu\}} + B_g(Q^2) \frac{\bar{P}^{\{\mu} i \sigma^{\nu\}\alpha} \Delta_\alpha}{2M} + C_g(Q^2) \frac{\Delta^\mu \Delta^\nu - g^{\mu\nu} \Delta^2}{M} \right] u(P, S)$$
In the forward limit ($Q^2 \to 0$):$A_g(0)$ corresponds to the Gluon Momentum Fraction $\langle x \rangle_g$.$J_g = \frac{1}{2}[A_g(0) + B_g(0)]$ gives the Total Gluon Angular Momentum.

To isolate the intrinsic gluon spin $\Delta G$, one must separate the total angular momentum into spin and orbital parts ($J_g = S_g + L_g$). While this separation is frame-dependent and technically subtle (often referred to as the Chen et al. or Jaffe-Manohar decomposition), the EMT provides the fundamental boundary condition for the total contribution.

% The total EMT of QCD is conserved ($\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$). Although the gluon-only part $T^{\mu\nu}_g$ is not separately conserved due to interaction with quarks, its scale evolution and renormalization are strictly governed by the Callan-Symanzik equations, allowing for a well-defined matching to experimental scales.

% The EMT serves as the "gravitational" map of the proton. It is possible to extract gluon spin because the off-diagonal components of the EMT ($T^{0k}$) describe the momentum density and flow of the color fields. By measuring how these fields respond to the nucleon's rotation (spin), and using Gradient Flow to tame the quantum fluctuations, we can non-perturbatively determine the gluon's share of the proton's angular momentum.
\subsection{Renormalization}
%  Traditionally, the gluon EMT is highly divergent on the lattice ($1/a^4$). By applying Symanzik/Wilson Flow, the gauge fields are smoothed in a 5th dimension (flow time $t$). The flowed operators $T_{\mu\nu}(t)$ are UV-finite and do not require the standard, noisy power-divergent subtraction.
To circumvent the traditional difficulties of gluon renormalization, we implement the Gradient Flow formalism. We introduce a flow time $t$ and define the flowed gauge field $B_\mu(x, t)$ through the diffusion-like equation:$$\frac{\partial B_\mu(x, t)}{\partial t} = D_\nu G_{\nu\mu}(x, t), \quad B_\mu(x, 0) = U_\mu(x)$$where $G_{\nu\mu}$ is the field strength tensor of the flowed field. The flow process systematically suppresses high-frequency gauge configurations, effectively "smearing" the field over a physical radius $r \approx \sqrt{8t}$.As shown by Lüscher, composite operators constructed from flowed fields $B_\mu$ at $t > 0$ are UV finite after the standard renormalization of the bare coupling. This remarkable property allows us to define the gluon spin operator on the lattice without the need for traditional, noisy power-divergent subtraction.
\subsection{Non-Perturbative Renormalization and NNLO Matching}
To connect our flowed lattice measurements to the standard $\overline{\text{MS}}$ scheme at a scale $\mu = 2$ GeV, we utilize the Small Flow-time Expansion (SFE). The relationship between the flowed operator $\mathcal{O}(t)$ and the renormalized continuum operator $\mathcal{O}^R(\mu)$ is given by:$$\mathcal{O}^R(\mu) = \lim_{t \to 0} \zeta(t, \mu) \mathcal{O}(t)$$Following the analytical breakthroughs in Harlander et al. (2018) (as seen in coefemt.pdf), we adopt the NNLO (two-loop) Wilson coefficients for the matching factor $\zeta(t, \mu)$. The coefficients are expanded in terms of the flowed coupling $\alpha_s(q)$ at a scale $q = 1/\sqrt{8t}$.By performing the measurements at multiple flow times ($t/a^2 \in [0.1, 0.6]$), we perform a controlled extrapolation to the $t \to 0$ limit. This procedure ensures that we stay within the "linear flow window"—large enough to suppress lattice artifacts but small enough for the perturbative SFE to remain valid.

\subsection{The Gluon Spin Operator}
The gluon spin contribution $\Delta G$ is related to the matrix element of the polarized gluon operator. In the continuum, this is often expressed through the Chern-Simons current or the field strength tensor $F_{\mu\nu}$. On the lattice, we construct the field strength tensor using the Clover-leaf discretization:$$\hat{F}_{\mu\nu}(x) = \frac{1}{8a^2} \text{Im} \left( \sum_{i=1}^4 Q_{i, \mu\nu}(x) \right)$$where $Q_{i, \mu\nu}$ represents the four $1 \times 1$ plaquettes originating from point $x$ in the $\mu-\nu$ plane. While this operator is standard, its direct measurement is hampered by $1/a^4$ power divergences and heavy mixing.

In practice, the standard $1 \times 1$ clover-leaf discretization is sufficient for constructing the field strength tensor $F_{\mu\nu}$. This is justified by the fact that the Gradient Flow effectively suppresses short-distance (UV) fluctuations and significantly reduces $O(a^2)$ discretization errors, a procedure widely adopted in recent lattice studies of $\Delta G$ and the energy-momentum tensor.

\subsection{3pt}
In our calculation, we employ a global-source sampling strategy to enhance the signal-to-noise ratio. The nucleon two-point functions are pre-computed as a comprehensive tensor $C_{2pt}(n, \Delta t, t_0)$, where $n$ denotes the gauge configuration and $t_0 \in [0, N_T-1]$ spans all possible temporal source locations.For the zero-momentum $(\vec{p}=\vec{0})$ three-point functions of the EMT and spin operators, the contraction is performed by:

$$C_{3pt}^{ij}(\Delta t, \tau) = \frac{1}{N_T} \sum_{t_0=0}^{N_T-1} \left\langle \mathcal{N}_i(\vec{p}=0; t_0 + \Delta t, t_0) \cdot \mathcal{A}_j(\vec{q}=0; t_0 + \tau) \right\rangle$$
where the sink time is $t_{sink} = (t_0 + \Delta t) \pmod{N_T}$ and the current insertion time is $t_{curr} = (t_0 + \tau) \pmod{N_T}$. By implementing this cyclic convolution across the entire temporal extent, we effectively utilize the translational invariance of the lattice and achieve a significant reduction in statistical uncertainty. For the region where $\tau > \Delta t$, the values are set to zero to maintain a consistent data alignment for subsequent joint-fit analysis.
