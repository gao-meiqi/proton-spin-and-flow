$$
\langle P'|T^{\mu\nu}_{q,g}|P\rangle .
$$

In principle, the corresponding form factors contain information about the angular momentum carried by quarks and gluons.

However, unlike the electromagnetic current,

$$
J^\mu ,
$$

there is no known fundamental probe that couples directly to the QCD energy-momentum tensor(引力子).

As a result, EMT form factors cannot be measured in the same direct way as electromagnetic form factors.

# DVCS

One possible way around this difficulty is through generalized parton distributions (GPDs).

Experimentally, information related to EMT form factors can be extracted from off-forward matrix elements appearing in processes such as deeply virtual Compton scattering (DVCS),

$$
\gamma^* p \rightarrow \gamma p .
$$

In these processes, one studies matrix elements of two electromagnetic currents,

$$
\langle P'|\, J_\alpha J_\beta \,|P\rangle ,
$$

which define the hadronic tensor.

The corresponding form factors can then be related to EMT form factors and extrapolated to the forward limit.

# Lattice QCD

Lattice QCD provides a unique opportunity to access EMT matrix elements directly.

Instead of relying on experimental probes, one computes

$$
\langle P'|T^{\mu\nu}|P\rangle
$$

from Euclidean correlation functions.

In particular, three-point correlation functions with EMT operator insertions allow the extraction of the relevant form factors.

This makes lattice QCD one of the few first-principles approaches capable of determining the quark and gluon contributions to proton spin.
