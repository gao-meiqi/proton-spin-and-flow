#### Symmetry Reduction and Operator Mixing
In continuum Euclidean spacetime, the full $O(4)$ rotational symmetry dictates the renormalization structure of quantum fields. The symmetric, traceless energy-momentum tensor (EMT), $T_{\mu\nu}$, transforms as an irreducible rank-2 tensor under $O(4)$, preventing it from mixing with lower-dimensional operators of different spin structures during renormalization.However, the introduction of a discrete spacetime lattice breaks the continuous $O(4)$ symmetry down to the isometry group of a four-dimensional hypercube, namely the hypercubic group $H(4)$. Under this restricted symmetry, the continuum irreducible representations (irreps) subduce into the irreps of $H(4)$.Specifically, consider the diagonal components of the bare gluonic EMT, such as the energy density operator:$$T_{44}^{\text{bare}} = G_{4\rho}^a G_{4\rho}^a - \frac{1}{4} G_{\rho\sigma}^a G_{\rho\sigma}^a$$Unlike the off-diagonal components (e.g., $T_{4i}$ with $i=1,2,3$), which transform under the 6-dimensional irrep $\tau_3^{(6)}$ of $H(4)$ and remain protected from lower-dimensional mixing, the diagonal component $T_{44}$ contains a piece that transforms under the trivial singlet representation $A_1$ (the identity representation) of $H(4)$. This is because $T_{44}$ invariant under all discrete lattice rotations and reflections, sharing identical transformation properties with the identity operator $\mathbb{I}$ (the vacuum).
#### Dimensional Analysis and Power Divergences
According to the principles of the Operator Product Expansion (OPE) and lattice renormalization theory, any bare operator $O_D$ of engineering dimension $D$ can mix under renormalization with any lower-dimensional operator $O_d$ (where $d \le D$) that shares the exact same quantum numbers and lattice symmetry representations:$$O_D^{\text{ren}} = Z \left[ O_D^{\text{bare}} + \sum_{d < D} \frac{C_d}{a^{D-d}} O_d \right]$$where $a$ is the lattice spacing, serving as the hard ultraviolet (UV) cutoff $\Lambda_{\text{UV}} \sim 1/a$.For the gluonic energy density component:The field strength tensor $G_{\mu\nu} \sim \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu + \dots$ has a mass dimension of 2. Thus, the EMT operator $T_{44}$ has an engineering dimension of $D = 4$.The identity operator $\mathbb{I}$ represents the vacuum state and carries a mass dimension of $d = 0$.Substituting these dimensions into the mixing formula yields the power-divergence index $\Delta = D - d = 4 - 0 = 4$. Consequently, the renormalized operator must take the following structural form:$$T_{44}^{\text{ren}} = Z_{44} \left[ T_{44}^{\text{bare}} + \frac{C_{\mathbb{I}}}{a^4} \mathbb{I} + \mathcal{O}(a^{-2}) O_2 + \dots \right]$$The coefficient $C_{\mathbb{I}}/a^4$ represents a quartic power divergence. As the continuum limit is approached ($a \to 0$), this term diverges at a catastrophic rate of $\mathcal{O}(a^{-4})$.
#### Phenomenological Consequence: Signal-to-Noise Severance
In practical lattice QCD simulations, extracting the gluonic matrix elements within a proton state $|P\rangle$ requires computing the hadronic three-point correlation functions. The physical signal we aim to measure is the forward matrix element $\langle P | T_{44}^{\text{ren}} | P \rangle$, which scales as the proton mass scale:$$\langle P | T_{44}^{\text{ren}} | P \rangle \sim \mathcal{O}(1 \text{ GeV}^4)$$However, evaluating the bare operator directly exposes the calculation to the vacuum expectation value (VEV) driven by the identity mixing term:$$\langle P | \frac{C_{\mathbb{I}}}{a^4} \mathbb{I} | P \rangle = \frac{C_{\mathbb{I}}}{a^4} \langle P | P \rangle$$On a typical fine lattice with $a \approx 0.05 \text{ fm}$, the lattice cutoff scale corresponds to $\Lambda_{\text{UV}} \sim 4 \text{ GeV}$. The quartic divergence introduces a background noise level of:$$\frac{1}{a^4} \sim (4 \text{ GeV})^4 \approx 256 \text{ GeV}^4$$This implies that the lattice artifact noise originating from the UV cutoff is two to three orders of magnitude larger than the actual QCD bound-state physical signal.
